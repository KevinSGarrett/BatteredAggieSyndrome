# Research roadmap

This roadmap describes acceptance-driven research goals, not promised shipping dates. More complex models do not take priority over correct evidence and evaluation.

## 1. Correctness and reproducibility

Complete source and transformation audits; resolve current-opponent binding and historical/current feature semantics; independently reconstruct metrics; and make probability/interval consistency testable. Preserve failures and unresolved evidence explicitly.

Acceptance means the audited scope is named, independently checked, and reproducible—not that every historical claim is assumed correct.

## 2. National forecast evaluation

Evaluate simple national baselines under chronological splits. Demonstrate usable input coverage, meaningful abstention rules, calibration, interval coverage, and error distributions. Freeze prospective forecasts before games and retain all eligible results, including bad ones.

Acceptance requires evidence for the stated population and uncertainty, without using evaluation outcomes to select and then re-evaluate the same candidate.

## 3. A&M and comparable-program analysis

Test whether any A&M residual pattern persists across seasons, peer definitions, and expectation models. Separate descriptive associations from causal explanations. Report null and unstable findings.

Acceptance requires repeated national comparisons and sensitivity analysis; one game does not establish BAS.

## 4. Public research reports

Develop understandable reports combining expectations, uncertainty, data completeness, and postgame residuals. Add team-score projections, player-impact scenarios, or more complex models only when their additional data and validation requirements are met.

Acceptance requires a report whose visible claims match the exact evidence and validated scope behind it. Attractive dashboards and large simulation counts are not substitutes for this evidence.
