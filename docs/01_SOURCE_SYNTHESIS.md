## Evolution of project intent
1. The project began as calibrated probabilistic CFB forecasting using broad national data rather than A&M-only training.
2. It expanded into matchup-aware play-by-play/player/coaching intelligence and explicit PIT leakage controls.
3. BAS evolved from a themed score into a scientific underperformance target based on leakage-safe expected margin.
4. Data strategy shifted toward accessible public sources, with PFF/SIS becoming non-core and an early pretrained-model claim later explicitly withdrawn as unverified.
5. Travel, schedule stress, empirical feature governance, current-team state, player/replacement availability and early-season uncertainty became core architecture.
6. A comprehensive gap audit added possession, field position, special teams, fourth-down/clock, score state, opponent adjustment, rule eras, officiating, uncertainty/OOD, joint-score consistency, lineage and replay/error analysis.
7. Historical weighting separated general football learning from direct current-A&M evidence, while FCS/lower divisions were bounded through hierarchical resolution.
8. Texas A&M specialization became a first-class layer with deeper team/player/unit/coaching data, calibration, evaluation, snapshots and postmortems.
9. Program resources became conditional upstream priors/expectation variables with public/private asymmetry and double-counting controls.
10. The end-state became an autonomous but governed MLOps loop; deterministic tasks remain deterministic, and research agents cannot rewrite their judging rules.
11. Core compute remains local-first; advanced neural/graph/sequence research is later and must beat strong conventional baselines.
